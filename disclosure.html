Disclosure & Editorial Policy

How this site makes money, and what that means for you.
Short version: Some of the tools I recommend are affiliate partners. If you click my link and sign up or buy, I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. I only recommend tools I personally use in my businesses or have directly verified work. The commission never decides what I recommend.

What "affiliate" actually means

An affiliate link is a special tracked URL. When you click it and become a customer, the company pays me a referral fee. The price you pay is the same whether you use my link, a different link, or no link at all. The fee comes out of the company's margin, not your wallet.

Some links on this site, in my emails, in the PDFs I publish, in my social posts, in my videos, and in my DMs are affiliate links. I label them — usually with "(paid link)" right next to the link, or with a clear note at the top of an email or post.

The Operator Filter

Every tool I recommend has to pass what I call the Operator Filter:

  • It must be used live inside one of my three businesses (Celebrate in 360, Revenue Reclaimer, or my real-estate operation), OR
  • I have to have personally tested it to a level where I'd recommend it to a friend without any commission.

A high commission doesn't get a tool past the Filter. A low commission doesn't keep a great tool out. If something I recommend later turns out to be bad, I update the recommendation and tell you why.

How I disclose, channel by channel

I work in a lot of formats. Here's how the disclosure shows up in each one, so you always know what you're looking at.

  • This website: a persistent disclosure in the footer of every page, a banner above any section that contains recommendations, and "(paid link)" next to individual affiliate links.
  • Email / newsletter: a heads-up at the top of any email that contains paid links, plus inline notes near each link.
  • PDFs / blueprints / lead magnets: a notice on the cover or first content page, plus inline notes.
  • Social posts (Instagram, Facebook, Threads, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, Substack Notes): a clear disclosure in the post itself — words like ad, sponsored, paid, or paid partnership — placed before any "see more" cutoff. Not buried in the bio.
  • Videos (TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts, long-form YouTube): both spoken out loud AND shown on screen within the first 15 seconds, plus a note in the description.
  • Podcasts I host or guest on: verbal disclosure during the segment, plus a note in the show notes.
  • Automated DMs (ManyChat funnel): the disclosure goes inside the same DM that delivers the link.

Paid sponsorships (different from affiliate)

Sometimes a brand pays a flat fee for a feature instead of a commission. When that happens I say "sponsored" explicitly, name the brand, and only accept the sponsorship if the product would pass the Operator Filter without the check. Opinions stay mine.

What I will not do

  • I will not recommend something I haven't used or verified.
  • I will not hide a disclosure in tiny font, light text, or below the fold.
  • I will not put the only disclosure in a profile bio while the post itself has none.
  • I will not stuff #aff or other vague abbreviations into a post and call it disclosed — the FTC has been explicit that "affiliate link" alone doesn't tell consumers what's happening.
  • I will not let a high commission decide a recommendation.

Earnings disclaimer

I share what I've done in my businesses and what I've seen others do. I make no guarantee that you will earn any specific amount of money — or any money at all — from following my content, using a tool I recommend, or buying anything I sell. Your results depend on your market, your effort, your existing skills, your execution, and dozens of other factors I can't control.

Most people who buy education or follow business content do not make significant money from it. Any numbers I cite — my own results, a member's result, a projection — should be considered exceptional, not typical. When I project what's possible (e.g., "$500K Year 1"), I'm describing what could happen if every part of a plan is executed. It's a model, not a promise.

Standards I follow

This policy is built around the FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, updated 2023), the FTC's Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers guidance, and the FTC's Money-Making Opportunity Rule (16 CFR Part 437). I review this page quarterly and update it whenever FTC guidance changes.
