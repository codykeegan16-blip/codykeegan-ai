Domain Expiration Arbitrage

Ten thousand domains expire every day. Some are worth thousands. AI knows which.

The hook: Right now, somebody is about to lose a $40,000 domain because they forgot to pay $12.

What it is

Every day, roughly ten thousand domains expire. Most are junk. A handful are not — exact-match-keyword domains, brandable names, expired businesses with backlink profiles, .ai and .co domains that someone forgot to renew.

Domain auction sites already monetize this. But the cycle of "appraise → buy → flip → repeat" is still mostly manual. Operators with AI can move faster, evaluate cheaper, and surface picks before the auction crowd notices.

It's not get-rich-quick. It's get-paid-attention-to-a-feed-most-people-ignore.

How the workflow runs

  1. A daily pull of the expired-domain auction feeds (Dropcatch, NameJet, GoDaddy Auctions, Park.io all publish them).
  2. A language model scores each one: keyword value, brandability, .com vs .net vs .io, prior-use red flags, SEO history.
  3. High-scoring picks get flagged to a daily digest.
  4. Manual review of the digest takes 5 minutes. Bids placed on the top 1-3 picks.
  5. Either flip (Sedo, Afternic, Flippa) for $500-5,000, or rent/lease the better ones to operators who actually want to build there.

Why this works now

AI evaluation is now cheaper than human evaluation, and faster. The auction window is short — the operator who reviews picks at 6 AM beats the operator who reviews at 9. Domain inventory is constant: there's always a fresh batch tomorrow.

Operator Filter take

Operator Filter: this one passes only if you treat it as a discipline, not a gamble. You're not picking lottery tickets. You're applying a consistent scoring framework to a recurring feed, taking the top 1-2 percent, and reselling slowly. It compounds with reputation — once you're known as someone who finds good domains, operators come to you.

What you'd need

  • Auction feed access (free for most, premium for some)
  • An AI scoring layer
  • A daily-digest delivery system
  • A bidding workflow (or an assistant who handles it)
  • Resale accounts on Sedo / Afternic / Flippa
