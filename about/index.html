Why this site exists

There is a gap right now between two kinds of people talking about AI.

On one side, tech people explain how AI works — the models, the architectures, the benchmarks. They are right about a lot of things. They almost never tell you where the money is.

On the other side, marketers tell you AI will change everything, buy this course, the future is here. They are usually right that something is changing. They almost never tell you what an actual operator should do on Monday morning.

I sit in the middle. I run three businesses on AI right now. I've spent 23 years inside hundreds of others. I write this site for the operators in between.

The 23-year career that taught me the Operator Filter

I started selling in 2003. Cintas — uniforms and facility services. Five years walking into hundreds of businesses every week. Medical offices. Manufacturing floors. Law firms. Restaurants. Contractors. Every kind of operator, every kind of broken system. I watched what made money and what didn't from inside the door, not from inside a deck.

After that, broadcast advertising. Then seven figures on Amazon, Etsy, and eBay through a product line that earned an AARP Must-Have Product of the Year award in 2019. Then a 360-degree photo booth business serving hundreds of events. Then real estate. Then a GoHighLevel agency for service businesses.

The pattern across every one of them: the businesses that win don't have better tools. They have better systems. The tool is the easy part. The system is the moat.

After enough years inside enough businesses, you stop predicting. You start recognizing. You've already seen the same pattern work or fail somewhere else.

That recognition is what I call the Operator Filter. Every tool I recommend on this site has to pass it: used live in one of my three businesses, or personally verified by direct testing. Not theory. Not demos. Not "this should work." Real money, real workflows, on real days.
